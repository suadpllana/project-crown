# Sources, provenance and ground-truth evidence

## Scientific sources

* D. York, N. M. Evensen, M. Lopez Martinez, J. De Basabe Delgado, "Unified equations for
  the slope, intercept, and standard errors of the best straight line", *Am. J. Phys.* 72,
  367 (2004). The regression algorithm, and standard errors evaluated at adjusted points.
* D. York, *Can. J. Phys.* 44, 1079 (1966); D. York, *Earth Planet. Sci. Lett.* 5, 320
  (1969). The original least-squares line with errors in both coordinates and correlated
  errors.
* K. Pearson, *Phil. Mag.* 2, 559 (1901): the (x, y) benchmark data, with the weights
  introduced by York (1966).
* A. H. Jaffey et al., *Phys. Rev. C* 4, 1889 (1971): 238U and 235U decay constants.
* J. Hiess, D. J. Condon, N. McLean, S. R. Noble, *Science* 335, 1610 (2012): 238U/235U
  = 137.818 in terrestrial zircon.
* G. W. Wetherill, *Trans. Am. Geophys. Union* 37, 320 (1956): the concordia diagram and the
  episodic Pb-loss interpretation.
* F. Tera and G. J. Wasserburg, *Earth Planet. Sci. Lett.* 14, 281 (1972): the
  Tera-Wasserburg diagram.
* K. R. Ludwig, *Isoplot 3.75* (Berkeley Geochronology Center Spec. Publ. 5, 2012), and
  P. Vermeesch, *Geosci. Front.* 9, 1479 (2018) (IsoplotR). Standard practice for discordia
  intercepts and the sqrt(MSWD) over-dispersion convention.
* Pearson-York reference output: the R package *bfsl* by P. Sturm (CRAN; README example
  `bfsl(pearson_york)`) reports intercept 5.47991 (SE 0.29497), slope -0.48053
  (SE 0.05799), chi-square 11.87 on 8 degrees of freedom, goodness of fit 1.483.

## What comes from the literature and what was designed here

The equations (background.md) are standard and are taken from the sources above. The
following are original to this task:

* splitting the LA-ICP-MS workflow into conversion, regression, intercepts, intercept
  uncertainties and integration;
* the explicit age window and root-completeness requirement;
* the MSWD convention as stated;
* the function contracts and all tests, including the independent check pipeline.

No public implementation was copied.

One literature discrepancy is noted for transparency. The bfsl output quoted above gives
cov(a, b) = -0.01651. That value equals -Xbar var_b with Xbar the weighted mean of the
**observed** abscissae. The York et al. (2004) definition used in this task,
-x_adj_bar var_b with **adjusted** abscissae, gives -0.016473. It is consistent with the
published intercept standard error 0.29497 = sqrt(1/sum W + x_adj_bar^2 var_b). For this
reason the tests check the published a, b, sigma_a, sigma_b and MSWD, but not the bfsl
covariance.

## Data used by the tests

No external files and no stored targets (`test_data.h5`) are needed. Every input is
embedded in, or generated deterministically by, the test files.

* **Pearson-York benchmark (published data).** Ten points with weights w_x, w_y
  (sx = 1/sqrt(w_x), sy = 1/sqrt(w_y), rho = 0).
* **Synthetic zircon U-Pb data (step 5 and general.py).** These are synthetic, generated by
  `_synthetic_tw(seed, n, t_upper, t_lower, rel_sX, rel_sY, rho_tw, dispersion)` in the
  tests using numpy `default_rng` seeds 0, 11, 21, 31, 101, 202, 303 and 404. True points lie
  on the chord between concordia points C(t_lower) and C(t_upper), at Pb-loss fractions
  evenly spaced between 0.15 and 0.95. They are converted to Tera-Wasserburg ratios and
  assigned 1-sigma errors of 1.0-1.5 % (X) and 0.6-1.0 % (Y) with rho_TW between -0.2 and
  0.15. Correlated Gaussian noise of 0, 0.4, 1, 1.8 or 2.5 times the stated errors is then
  added, which gives MSWD values from 0 (collinear) through about 0.1-0.6 (under-dispersed)
  to about 6.6 (over-dispersed).

  These choices reproduce typical LA-ICP-MS zircon data: an Archean (2700 Ma) population with
  Palaeozoic (450 Ma) Pb loss, a Palaeoproterozoic (1850 Ma) population with recent (0 Ma)
  or Palaeozoic (300 Ma) Pb loss, and others. The resulting intercept uncertainties of
  4-20 Ma are typical of published discordia ages from such data.
* **Constructed lines (steps 3 and 4).** Lines through two exactly computed concordia points
  (including t = 0, negative ages and ages beyond the window), lines of negative slope, and
  lines shifted off the curve.

## Ground-truth evidence

None of the expected values is produced by the reference solution.

| Target | Independent evidence | Limitations |
|---|---|---|
| Wetherill ratios and errors | Derivation in relative (logarithmic) errors; analytic special case rho_TW = 0; exact round trip through the inverse transformation | First-order propagation by definition |
| York a, b, sigma_a, sigma_b, MSWD on Pearson-York | Published values (5 significant digits) | Checked to the published precision (+-6e-6) |
| York fit on correlated data | Maximum-likelihood line found by direct grid plus golden-section minimisation of S(b) (a different algorithm); covariance from adjusted points obtained by geometric Mahalanobis projection | Agreement better than 1e-8 |
| York special cases | Weighted least squares in closed form when sx -> 0; exactly collinear data; axis-swap symmetry (slope -> 1/b, sigma_b -> sigma_b/b^2, same MSWD); exact scaling laws when all errors are scaled | |
| Intercept ages | Lines constructed through known concordia ages (exact by construction); dense grid scan with bisection | Tangent (double-root) lines not tested |
| Intercept-age sigmas | Central finite differences of numerically found roots with respect to a and b, propagated with the full covariance | Linearisation, consistent with the stated first-order convention |
| Integrated results | Independent pipeline built from all of the above, plus the sqrt(MSWD) rule; seeded data recover the generating ages within 4 sigma | Statistical check uses fixed seeds, so it is deterministic |

## Validation performed

* Per-step references pass their tests, and solution.py passes general.py.
* second_solution.py passes every test file. It uses log-space propagation, a direct
  maximum-likelihood fit with geometric adjusted points, root finding in
  s = exp(lambda_238 t), and centroid re-parametrisation.
* Every scaffold fails. All 20 negative-control mutants fail:
  * stub, None, zeros, wrong shapes;
  * TW correlation ignored, 238U/235U = 137.88, Wetherill correlation dropped;
  * OLS, York without rho, York errors at observed points, MSWD with n degrees of freedom,
    cov sign flipped;
  * single Newton root, descending order, no age window;
  * sigma without covariance, sigma in wrong units;
  * deflation when MSWD < 1, no inflation, single intercept accepted.
* Tests are deterministic and offline, use only numpy, and each file runs in about 1 s.
